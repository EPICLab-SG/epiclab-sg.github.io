Roles & principles

Good research depends on shared principles. At EPIC Lab, we want everyone to understand their responsibilities, the support they can expect, and how we work together to help each other grow.

Our roles reflect different stages of a research career, from a first experience of research to leading an independent programme.

  • Principal investigator
  • Postdoctoral researcher
  • PhD student
  • Research students (URECA, Interns, FYP)

Working together

Across all roles, our shared values are to insist on high standards, take initiative, show respect and earn trust, and communicate effectively. Our working practices are intended to support focused research, collaboration, and honest discussion of progress and difficulties.

Working hours, flexibility, and being present

As a general rule, contact hours are 9am to 5pm. Research is a creative pursuit, and concentrated work can be demanding: you can adjust your schedule and finish early when you have done enough for the day and need to rest.

Lab members are encouraged to be in the office at least two days a week to support in-person collaboration and relationships with peers. Working from outside Singapore requires a request and a genuine need; remote working flexibility does not replace official leave.

Meetings and preparation

Project meetings are usually online to include international collaborators; please have a functioning digital writing pad so that we can work through ideas together. Group meetings are in person by default, with alternative arrangements during conference periods.

Before each meeting, send short notes answering:

  1. What have you done since our last meeting?
  2. What would you like to discuss or demonstrate this time?
  3. Where are you stuck, and what do you think the answer might be and why?

The purpose is to help you reflect and arrive ready to think together. Ad hoc meetings are welcome when you have significant progress to share, are stuck in your research, or would like to discuss difficulties in life.

Training and the research community

Participate in reading groups, lab meetings and presentations, and relevant machine learning summer or winter schools. When travelling to a conference, look for opportunities to combine the trip with a research visit and a presentation of your work.

Principal investigator / group leader

The principal investigator (PI) is responsible for building a research environment in which people can grow and carry out deep, original, and impactful work. This means taking responsibility for both the scientific direction of the lab and its research culture.

Key responsibilities
  • Define, sustain, and advance a coherent research programme that addresses timely and important scientific questions.
  • Recruit outstanding researchers who raise the group's intellectual standards.
  • Provide a clear scientific vision and strategic direction.
  • Support researchers' development through mentorship, feedback, and guidance.
  • Be accountable for the quality, integrity, and long-term development of the group's research output and culture.

Postdoctoral researcher

A postdoc is an early-career researcher developing the experience, skills, and intellectual independence needed for an academic career or other careers centred on research. At EPIC, this is a stage for building your own research profile and taking the lead on projects aligned with the group's research agenda.

Key responsibilities
  • Devote a substantial portion of your time to developing your own research profile and leading research projects.
  • Show increasing independence, maturity, and originality, moving beyond a direct continuation of your PhD work.
  • Produce high-quality, deep research that advances the field, evidenced through peer-reviewed publications and other recognised scholarly outputs. This is the primary basis for evaluating your research performance.
  • Support the training and mentoring of junior PhD students, and contribute your own ideas to the direction of shared projects.

PhD student

A PhD is training in how to produce new knowledge. Alongside contributing to research, you will develop the expertise, judgement, and communication skills needed for an academic career or other careers centred on research.

Key responsibilities

Your PhD project belongs to you. With your supervisor's guidance, you take primary responsibility for driving the research forward through new insights, discoveries, or theoretical developments.

  • Develop at least one set of deep technical and methodological skills, and deep expertise in at least one research domain.
  • Take ownership of the research process and contribute new knowledge.
  • Build a professional academic network.
  • Learn to communicate your contributions clearly and explain how they relate to existing research.
  • Contribute to the university's teaching activities as part of your academic training.

Your supervisor provides mentorship, feedback, and strategic guidance. You should use that support to develop your own ideas and judgement; taking the next research step remains your responsibility.

Research students (URECA, Interns, FYP)

A student research project is an opportunity to learn what research involves through hands-on experience, whether through URECA, an internship, or a final-year project (FYP). You join the lab as a trainee, supporting ongoing work and taking on a small research project of your own.

Key responsibilities
  • Support the group through activities such as reviewing literature and analysing data.
  • Explore a small research project of your own as a way to learn the research process.
  • Reflect on setbacks: understand why an approach did not work, learn from it, and try again with a better idea.

Learning comes first. You are not expected to produce original, publishable work unless you choose to pursue it. Setbacks and false starts are a normal part of learning to do research.
